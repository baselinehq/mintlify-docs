RunPod is a GPU cloud that bills GPU work per day, with on-demand pods and
serverless endpoints broken down by GPU type.

CostGraph reads RunPod as **FOCUS 1.2** records at a grain of one record per pod
or serverless endpoint per GPU type per day.

## Choose how the data reaches us

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="CostGraph pulls it" icon="cloud-arrow-down">
    You supply read-only credentials and we call RunPod once a day. Nothing to run.
  </Card>
  <Card title="You push it" icon="cloud-arrow-up">
    You send us FOCUS records over the API. Credentials never leave your infrastructure.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Let CostGraph pull

1. Open **Settings -> Integrations** and pick **RunPod**.
2. Name the connection and pick the tenant these charges belong to.
3. Fill in the values the form asks for. It lists exactly the variables RunPod
   needs, marks which are optional, and explains the permission each one requires.
4. Select **Connect**.

CostGraph verifies the credentials with a short fetch before saving, so a wrong
value or a missing permission fails while you are still on the form.

<Note>
Credentials are encrypted at rest and used only to read billing data. Revoke them in
RunPod at any time and the connection stops; nothing else is affected.
</Note>

## Push it yourself

Send the charges to CostGraph as FOCUS records instead. [Push FOCUS data](/costgraph/integrations/focus-push) covers the connection id,
the `focus:write` key, and the request shape.

You do not have to write the converter yourself.
[Run the exporter](/costgraph/integrations/exporter) in your own environment with
`--provider runpod` and it reads this provider and pushes the records for you.

## What CostGraph does with it

Records land as raw billing rows, are normalised into line items, and roll up into
daily cost. From there RunPod spend appears in Cost Overview and in anomaly
detection alongside every other provider, so idle GPUs and stopped-pod storage
waste stand out.
