Convex is a reactive backend platform, billed as developer seats plus metered
backend usage.

CostGraph reads Convex as **FOCUS 1.2** records at a grain of one record per team
per month, covering the developer seats on the plan.

## Choose how the data reaches us

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="CostGraph pulls it" icon="cloud-arrow-down">

  </Card>
  <Card title="You push it" icon="cloud-arrow-up">

## Before you begin

- In the Convex dashboard, open your user settings and create a **personal access
  token**. A team access token can't read your team's members, so it can't report
  seats.
- If your team is on negotiated pricing, have your per-developer rate to hand. We
  use Convex's published rate otherwise.

## Let CostGraph pull

1. Open **Settings > Integrations** and pick **Convex**.
2. Name the connection and pick the tenant these charges belong to.
3. Enter the values you copied. The form lists what Convex needs and marks the
   optional ones.
4. Select **Connect**.

CostGraph verifies the credentials with a short fetch before saving, so a wrong
value or a missing permission fails while you are still on the form.

<Note>
Credentials are encrypted at rest and used only to read billing data. Revoke them in
Convex at any time and the connection stops; nothing else is affected.
</Note>

## What you get

Seats are the part of a Convex bill that most often drifts: a team grows, people
move on, and the licence count stays. CostGraph reports the seats you're paying
for alongside the plan's included allowances, so headroom and waste sit next to
the charge.

Convex serves its metered usage only to a log stream, not to an API, so the seat
figure is the subscription rather than the full invoice. Overage on function
calls, storage and egress isn't included.

## Push it yourself

Send the charges to CostGraph as FOCUS records instead. [Push FOCUS data](/costgraph/integrations/focus-push) covers the connection id,
the `focus:write` key, and the request shape.

## Run the exporter

Rather than build that request yourself, run our exporter. It reads Convex on a
schedule and pushes the records for you, wherever you want to run it: a container, a
Kubernetes `CronJob`, or a binary on a machine you already have.

```bash
focus-exporter --provider convex --sink costgraph
```

Your Convex credential stays where you put it, and only the finished FOCUS records
reach CostGraph. [Run the exporter](/costgraph/integrations/exporter) covers the three
ways to run it and the variables it reads.

## What CostGraph does with it

Records land as raw billing rows, are normalised into line items, and roll up into
daily cost. From there Convex spend appears in Cost Overview and in anomaly
detection alongside every other provider.
